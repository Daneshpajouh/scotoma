\paperheading{Covering floor and minimum sound pattern families}
\label{sec:method}
\label{sec:construction}

Let $g=g_1\cdots g_n$ have maximal runs $R_0,\ldots,R_{r-1}$, run $k$ beginning at
$a_k$, and let $D_k$ delete one base of that run. Put $N=n-1$, $L=\{D_k:k<r\}$,
and $U_m=\bigcup_{P\in L}B_m(P)$. Write $K(N,r,m)$ for the minimum number of radius-$m$
balls covering $U_m$, with centres anywhere in the four-letter space. The coordinate isometry
below makes this depend only on $N,r,m$. The companion submission studies unrestricted covering and its fractional relaxation
\citep{companion_reach}. We first prove the elementary floor
needed at the deployed budget, then the minimum under soundness.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:structure}
$D_k[p]=g_p$ for $p<a_k$ and $D_k[p]=g_{p+1}$ for $p\ge a_k$.
Thus \emph{(i)} $d(D_k,D_j)=|k-j|$; \emph{(ii)} coordinate $p$ is informative exactly when
$g_p\ne g_{p+1}$. Ordering these $r-1$ coordinates first and relabelling their
two values gives $D_k=1^k0^{r-1-k}$ there, followed by \emph{(iii)} $N-r+1$ constant zeros:
the canonical word $P_k=1^k0^{N-k}$, in the same index $k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Deletion fixes the prefix and shifts the suffix left. Two deletion words differ
at exactly the intervening run boundaries. Each coordinate switches once as
$k$ increases, giving the staircase.
\end{proof}
This structure is published \citep{abusini2021reconstruction,abbasian2024errorball};
its relabelling leaves letters $2,3$ unused at every coordinate.

\begin{theorem}[A scattered transversal pins the covering number]\label{thm:floor}
Let $S$ be a finite word set. A \emph{$2m$-scattered transversal} chooses
$x_P\in B_m(P)$ for each $P\in S$, with $d(x_P,x_Q)>2m$ for $P\ne Q$.
If one exists, every radius-$m$ ball cover of $\bigcup_{P\in S}B_m(P)$
has at least $|S|$ balls, and $S$ attains that minimum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every representative must be covered; a ball containing two would put them within
$2m$. This is the unit-weight covering bound. Distant representatives are a published
notion \citep{fiala2005distant}; their existence is sufficient, not necessary.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The constructive range]\label{thm:general}
Every four-letter spacer of length $n\ge20$ admits such a transversal for every
integer $0\le m\le5$. Hence $K(n-1,r,m)=r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At $m=0$ take the library. Otherwise, in canonical coordinates, overwrite
$\tau_k(s)=1+((k+s-1)\bmod19)$ in $P_k$, using the first $m$ offsets
$(4,6,9,12,16)$ with letters $(2,3,2,3,2)$. Each witness is at distance $m$
from its pattern. The exact distance formulas give pairwise distance at least
$2m+1$ in the nineteen cyclic cases; the appendix supplies the formulas and tables,
including same-letter overlaps. For separation $t\ge19$, only the at most $m$
shared overwrites can cancel a differing boundary coordinate, so distance is at least
$t-m\ge2m+1$. Coordinates outside the cycle only add. The coordinate isometry
and restriction to the $r$ patterns finish the proof.
\end{proof}

This elementary construction alone settles the deployed budget $m=4$ and every
$m\le5$. The companion's weighting theorem extends the same equality through
$m\le9$ \citep{companion_reach}: it assigns fractional mass where one separated
representative per pattern no longer suffices.
That extension, $K(n-1,r,m)=r$ for every $n\ge20$, every run count and $0\le m\le9$, is
\emph{not} a result of this paper and is left unnumbered here. It, the full-weight stopping
budgets, the fractional thresholds, the integer obstructions including $K(19,11,10)=11$ with
$9<\tau^*(19,11,10)<10$, and the infinite families are the companion's, cited and not claimed
\citep{companion_reach}; this paper's own are the elementary transversal floor through $m\le5$,
the minimum sound pattern family theorem, the deficit law and the tool and assay measurements.

\subsection{Soundness, pooling and burst scope}

\begin{theorem}[The bound reaches the patterns an enumerator emits, per guide]\label{thm:sound}
Fix a spacer, let $L$ be its complete single-deletion library with $\lvert L\rvert=r$, let $U$ be the
union of the radius-$m$ balls around $L$, and let $m\le N-2$. Call a pattern \emph{sound} when every
word it accepts lies in $U$. Then at four letters no sound pattern carries a wildcard, and
the only sound centres are the $r$ members of $L$, so the fewest sound patterns whose acceptances
cover $U$ is exactly $r$, attained by $L$ and by nothing else.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each coordinate uses at most two library letters. If a pattern has a wildcard at $j$, write an
unused letter there and at $m$ other coordinates. It accepts the result within its budget,
but the result is at distance at least $m+1$ from every library word. A centre already carrying
unused letters at $s\ge1$ coordinates similarly escapes by changing $\min(m,N-s)$ others.
Thus a sound centre uses only library letters and is binary on the informative coordinates.
If it is unsorted, choose $u<v$ with bits $0,1$. Every staircase word disagrees at $u$ or $v$.
Changing $m$ other coordinates to unused letters is possible because $m\le N-2$ and yields
an accepted word outside $U$. The sound centres are therefore exactly $L$.
Finally, for each $D_j$, keep its one or two neighbour-boundary coordinates fixed and change
$m$ other coordinates to unused letters. This point lies in its ball and outside every other
library ball. Each $D_j$ is indispensable, so $L$ is the unique minimum sound family.
\end{proof}
At $n=20$ the soundness hypothesis is $m\le17$, including the deployed
four-mismatch budget for every run count.
The appendix retains a single-implementation $3{,}697{,}920$-centre check; the proof does not rely on it.
\paragraph{Two minima and a sequence-only count test.}
Soundness forbids words outside $U$; unrestricted covering requires only covering $U$. At
$m=2$ the guide \texttt{ACGTA} has unrestricted minimum four and sound minimum five, one of
the smallest such separations within $m\le N-2$; the appendix gives the finite check and the
explicit cover, and the length ranges above are unchanged.

For every $n\ge20$ and every $m\le5$, by \Cref{thm:general}, fewer than $r$ distinct
wildcard-free centres cannot cover $U$ wherever placed; for budgets six through nine that
conclusion is the companion's \citep{companion_reach}. One pass computes $r$ before any scan. A count of at least $r$ is
insufficient, since duplicates do not enlarge the word set; alignment engines are tested by
equality of projected target words with the deletion set. The appendix compares generate-and-diff and
search-scheme tests and their costs.

The theorem is per guide. Pooled libraries can use all four letters at a coordinate
and admit sound wildcards. Nonzero false-positive budgets and pooled sound minima
remain open \citep{kucherov2005multiseed,renders2024lossless,gottlieb2025search};
the companion treats unrestricted pooling separately \citep{companion_reach}.

\begin{proposition}[Burst structure]\label{prop:bstructure}
For deletion of $g_i\cdots g_{i+b-1}$, $D_i[p]=g_p$ for $p<i$ and $g_{p+b}$
otherwise. Hence $D_i=D_j$ ($i<j$) exactly when $g_p=g_{p+b}$ throughout
$i\le p<j$, including \Cref{prop:deficit}'s adjacent collision condition.
Indexing distinct words by these changes gives $d(D_i,D_j)=|i-j|$; each coordinate
uses at most two letters and switches once. Thus \Cref{thm:floor} applies whenever
a transversal exists at length $N=n-b$. At $n=20,m=4$, $b=1$ follows from
\Cref{thm:general}. For $b=2,3$, set $N=20-b$ and overwrite canonical $P_k$ at
$1+((k+s-1)\bmod N)$, $s=(2,5,9,13)$, with letters $(2,3,2,3)$.
Each witness has radius four; literal Hamming checks of all $171$ pairs at
$N=18$ and $153$ at $N=17$ give minimum distance nine. Restricting the chain
settles every burst library. The appendix gives replay and panel structure
checks for $b=1$ to $5$; large $b$ can fail.
\end{proposition}

Contiguity matters: \texttt{AACAA} has four unrestricted two-deletion words but
covering number three at radius one; its single-deletion library has size and covering
number three. The appendix retains complete enumeration and support-search negatives:
construction failure at $m=5$ for $b=2,3$ does not refute the floor. The companion
treats insertion \citep{companion_reach}; no mixed-class floor is claimed.

\paperheading{Expansion counts and measured tool incompleteness}
\label{sec:reduction}
This section audits wildcard expansion within $m$ substitutions. A sequence occurring
$a$ times contributes $a$ genomic sites; both qualifying strands contribute two.
Pattern counts are set cardinalities. Unless stated otherwise, the census uses
NGG on both strands and excludes PAM bases from substitution distance.

\begin{proposition}[Deficit at every bulge size]\label{prop:deficit}
For $1\le b\le n-2$, the $b$-burst-deletion ball $B_b(g)$ deletes one contiguous
block. Its published size is $1+|\{i\in[1,n-b]:g_i\ne g_{i+b}\}|$
\citep{lan2026burstballs,schoeny2016burst,levenshtein1967adjacent}.
The wrapper \citep{park2016casoffinderbulge} emits $n-1$ of $n$ DNA patterns and
$1+|\{i\in[2,n-b-1]:g_i\ne g_{i+b}\}|$ RNA words. The RNA-word deficit is
$\Delta_{\rm RNA}=\mathbf{1}[g_1\ne g_{b+1}]+\mathbf{1}[g_n\ne g_{n-b}]$.
The DNA-pattern deficit is $\Delta_{\rm DNA}=1$, so the combined deficit is
$1+\Delta_{\rm RNA}\in\{1,2,3\}$: its leading one counts the lost DNA pattern.
Slot $0$ is degenerate for every $b$; wrapper RNA words retain $g_1,g_n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Adjacent block-deletion starts give the same word exactly when $g_i=g_{i+b}$;
the collision classes are contiguous. Counting the first word and then each change
gives the published ball-size identity. The wrapper retains only starts
$2,\ldots,n-b$, removing the first and last change terms. Its DNA loop retains
slots $1,\ldots,n-1$, while the complete non-degenerate set is $1,\ldots,n$.
Slot $0$ is byte-identical to the padded no-bulge pattern; slot $n$ places the
wildcard between spacer and PAM and is distinct. Thus one DNA pattern is always
lost, with the two endpoint indicators deciding the RNA loss. The appendix gives
both PAM orientations and the full source-loop derivation.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The guide in the clinic, and a one-mismatch ranking error.}

too low; their true counts are two for one locus and three for $49$, exactly the loci invisible

under-ranks the closest matches first. Replay at \path{code_release/clinical_guide/}.

\subsection{Measured endpoint losses and their denominators}
\label{ssec:measured-losses}
Activity here means a positive read count in the deposited assay table. Bulge
positions come from its alignment gaps, with gap counts checked against the
declared bulge counts. This is a measurement of nominations under a specified
search policy, rather than a cleavage mechanism inferred from an alignment.

At four mismatches, completing the $2016$ wrapper's patterns adds $3.98\%$
for single-DNA-bulge loci, $7.44\%$ for single-RNA-bulge loci and $6.40\%$ for
the bulge-attributable total. Each denominator is the audited wrapper's own
corresponding output count, rather than a complete locus count. Its omission excludes $129$ active in vitro sites/$40$
guides, all terminal RNA; the native parser library misses the same set, and both
have zero losses at six mismatches. This follows from \Cref{prop:deficit},
separately from the covering floor.
An independent implementation scanned all $4{,}936{,}278$ deposited rows under both
PAM policies, reproducing $583$ terminal annotations, $260$ exposed rows and zero
first-RNA and DNA losses. Its complete loss set agrees at row identity and multiplicity
on $129/40$ with exact NGG, both strands, PAM excluded from distance; admitting
non-NGG and charging PAM substitutions instead gives $148/43$. The $583/260$
annotation census retains deposited PAMs. Counts precede identity comparison,
without reference filtering; the deposited schema4 report and log record both
completed scans \citep{zw4_reconciliation_2026}.

\paragraph{Position, exposure and exclusive loss are different sets.}
Of $583$ terminal RNA annotations, $323$ have $g_n=g_{n-1}$: their terminal
word is emitted internally, and all are reached. The other $260$ have
$g_n\ne g_{n-1}$; $129$ evade every emitted pattern in budget on $40/110$ guides,
about half the exposed set and a fifth of the terminal set. Exposure is necessary,
not sufficient, for loss. The first RNA position and omitted DNA slot have no
measured-active losses in this panel.

For every one of the $129$, the terminal deletion is the unique
mismatch-minimising placement: an interior placement in budget would contradict
exclusivity. Scanning all NGG bulge-free registers on both strands of the deposited
window rescues none within four mismatches. At six mismatches every one is
reachable by an alternative bulged placement. The appendix's positional audit
retains the per-position counts, alignment checks and window controls.

\subsection{Exact counts and attribution}
\label{sec:lemmas}
Write $n=20$ for the spacer and $L=3$ for the PAM. A class with $d$ unpaired DNA bases and
$r$ unpaired RNA bases has pattern length $n+L+d-r$ and $L+d$ wildcards.

\begin{remark}[Classes and counting units]\label{rem:inv}
Unpadded length $n+L+d-r$ and wildcard count $L+d$ recover $(d,r)$, so classes
partition patterns. Their pattern counts add; locus counts require a computed
union, since classes can reach the same locus. Padded signatures do not separate
classes; the wrapper's pattern sets are instead disjoint (appendix).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Which guides collide, and how many patterns they share]\label{prop:ident}
Let $g \neq h$ be spacers of length $n$.
\emph{(i)} In an insertion-only class their pattern sets are disjoint: the whole spacer
survives in the pattern and is recovered by deleting the wildcards.
\emph{(ii)} In the single-deletion class the sets meet if and only if $g$ and $h$ are both
single-base insertions into one common $(n-1)$-word, that is, iff their single-deletion balls
intersect. That intersection has size at most $2$, Levenshtein's bound, not ours
\citep{levenshtein2001reconstruction}. For a one-base shift $h = x\,g_1\cdots g_{n-1}$ it
always contains $w = g_1\cdots g_{n-1}$, and a second word exactly when some $w',w''\in D_1(w)$ give
$xw' = w''g_n\ne w$, which needs periodicity at the junction ($\texttt{ACAC}$ and $\texttt{CACA}$ share
two). On this panel the intersection is empty for $5{,}990$ of $5{,}995$ pairs; for the five
one-base-shift pairs it is exactly $\{w\}$, in either direction.
\emph{(iii)} In the mixed class every shared $(n-1)$-word $w$ contributes exactly $n-1$ shared
patterns, namely the single-wildcard insertions into $w$ at slots $1,\ldots,n-1$; slot $0$ is
outside the class, placing the wildcard before the protospacer. A one-base-shift pair therefore shares exactly $n-1$, or $2(n-1)$ in that periodic case.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[DNA pattern count]\label{prop:count}
For one unpaired DNA base the scheme yields exactly $n$ distinct patterns per guide: of the $n+1$
insertion slots, slot $0$ is byte-identical to the run's own no-bulge pattern and contributes nothing,
and no two of the remaining $n$ coincide.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Panel-wide pattern count]\label{prop:panel}
For a panel of $M$ distinct spacers, with $D(G)$ the union of their single-deletion balls, the
scheme examines exactly $Mn$, $\lvert D(G)\rvert$ and $\lvert D(G)\rvert(n-1)$ distinct patterns
in the three classes, fixing its pattern count before any scan. Here $\lvert D(G)\rvert = 1{,}629$,
and deduplicating across guides would remove $0.31\%$ of single-RNA patterns: sharing is a correctness condition,
not a cost one. Proof in the appendix.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The cost of a stated bulge policy}
\label{ssec:bulge-cost}
On the $110$-guide CHANGE-seq panel on GRCh38, at four mismatches and NGG on both strands,
the baseline is $34{,}652$ ungapped loci. Merging bulged hits within $3$ bp of an ungapped
hit for the same guide and strand, one single-base bulge multiplies counts by $34.9$
panel-wide (median $40.1$), or $36.0$ without that merge ($1{,}212{,}681$ added
loci). The union through two bases costs $108.2$, the mixed DNA/RNA
class $187.8$, and the union of all three $276.1$, a $7.9$-fold policy spread (appendix).
The whole-number factors are $35$, $108$, $188$ and $276$. All four use the same
ungapped denominator and locus unions: the spread prices policy at fixed panel,
assembly, PAM and mismatch budget. The two-base policy allows one contiguous
bulge of either size; the mixed policy allows one DNA and one RNA bulge per site.
The assay deposits annotate only single bulges, so these prices do not establish
common two-base or mixed bulges among measured actives.
The bulge-free enumerator agrees with Cas-OFFinder \citep{bae2014casoffinder} in per-guide
count for all $110$ guides through five mismatches; the appendix holds the full taxonomy,
the separate-locus and two-tool bulge comparisons, counting-unit definitions and algorithms.
